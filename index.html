Hello, I am Seong-Min Kang, a Ph.D. student in Artificial Intelligence at Chung-Ang University. My work studies multimodal representation learning, text-video retrieval, and Video-LLM adaptation.

I am especially interested in representational usability: how pretrained features are aligned, preserved, accessed, and organized when models are transferred to video-language tasks. My recent work spans transfer-time optimization, representation geometry, contrastive learning, and diagnostic analysis of VLMs and LLMs.

Across my projects, I try to connect model behavior with practical retrieval and adaptation problems: making video-language systems more robust, more interpretable, and more useful under realistic transfer settings.

Research Contributions

LLM Diagnostics

Capacity without Access / DDI

Separated representational capacity from accessibility in LLM hidden states using spectral geometry, unembedding-subspace access, layer-wise similarity, and diagnostic intervention.

Video Retrieval

APT

Developed an image-to-video transfer framework with spatio-temporal token refinement, multi-scale token pooling, and alignment-preserving aggregation.

Graph Representation

DRGI

Modeled partial correspondence and hard negatives in text-video retrieval through factor-level graph representations and graph-infomax-based disentanglement.

Cross-Modal Learning

Dynamic Scale Position Embedding

Designed temporal position encoding for cross-modal representation learning, improving alignment between video dynamics and language descriptions.

Data Augmentation

Efficient Text Augmentation / MEME

Studied retrieval-oriented data augmentation through latent-space text augmentation and multi-encoder multi-expert modeling for video-language retrieval.

Representation Learning

Tree-Based Segmentation & CD-VAE

Explored hierarchical temporal organization for retrieval and extended contrastive disentangled representation learning to collaborative filtering.
